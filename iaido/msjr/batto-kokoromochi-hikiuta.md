## The position of iai

> 荒井清信公云、夫れ居合とは手近き勝負、柄口六寸の大事あり。其の形を事となし、前後左右是を知り、先ず居合抜き申す形は〔丸の中に三角〕如斯。
>
> 一、身を正しくして抜き申す形肝要也。
>
> 一、居合之位
>
> 面　薄櫻  
> 身之掛　鉢木　口傳  
> 心　水鳥

Arai Kiyonobu states:

Iai is combat at close quarters. There is the essential teaching of 柄口六寸 *tsukaguchi roku-sun* — “six *sun* at the mouth of the hilt”. Take its form as the matter at hand, know front, rear, left and right, and first draw in iai in the form shown here: **a triangle within a circle**.

It is essential to keep the body upright when drawing.

The position of iai:

- **Face:** pale cherry blossom.
- **Bearing of the body:** *Hachinoki*. Oral transmission.
- **Mind:** the waterbird.

## The mind of the waterbird

> 右の心、水鳥に気を付け工夫あるべし。古歌に、
>
> 帆を掛けて　急ぐ小舟に　乗らずとも  
> 行く水鳥の　心知るべし
>
> 水鳥の　水に住めども　羽は濡れず  
> 海の魚とて　汐はゆくなし

Concerning the foregoing mind, attend to the waterbird and investigate it thoroughly. An old poem says:

> Even without boarding a little boat  
> hastening onwards under sail,  
> understand the mind  
> of the waterbird as it goes.
>
> Though the waterbird dwells in water,  
> its feathers are not wetted;  
> though a fish lives in the sea,  
> the salt does not penetrate it.

## The mind of the white heron

> 居合太刀打共、白鷺心。口傳。古歌に、
>
> 忍ぶれど　色に出にけり　我が恋は  
> ものや思ふと　人の問ふまで
>
> 数ならぬ　心に身をば　任せねど  
> 身に従ふは　心なりけり

In both iai and sword engagement there is the **mind of the white heron** (白鷺心 *shirasagi-gokoro*). Oral transmission. An old poem says:

> Though I would conceal it,  
> my love has appeared in my expression,  
> until people ask me,  
> “Are you lost in thought?”
>
> Though I do not entrust my body  
> to a heart of no account,  
> it is nevertheless the heart  
> that follows the body.

## The great matter of the moon in water

> 居合太刀打共、水月之大事。口傳。古歌に、
>
> 水や空　空や水とも　見え分かず  
> 通ひて澄める　秋の夜の月
>
> おしなべて　ものを思はぬ　人にさへ  
> 心をつくる　秋の初風
>
> 秋来ぬと　目にはさやかに　見えねども  
> 風の音にぞ　驚かれぬる
>
> 右の心にて悟るべし。我は知らねども敵の掛を知らする也。其處、水月白鷺、習ひ有るべし。工夫すべし。

In both iai and sword engagement there is the **great matter of the moon in water** (水月之大事 *suigetsu no daiji*). Oral transmission. An old poem says:

> Water or sky,  
> sky or water — one cannot tell them apart;  
> the autumn-night moon passes freely through  
> and dwells in both.
>
> The first wind of autumn gives  
> even one who ordinarily thinks of nothing  
> a pensive heart.
>
> Though the eye cannot see clearly  
> that autumn has come,  
> the sound of the wind  
> startles me.

Awaken through this mind. Although I do not know it beforehand, it makes the enemy’s **掛 *kake*** — his coming-on, engagement or initiative — known.[^kake] Therein are the teachings of the moon in water and the white heron. Practise and investigate them.

## Winning by following the enemy

> 敵に随って勝つべき心持。古歌に、
>
> 風吹けば　柳の糸の　片寄りに  
> 靡くに付けて　過ぐる春かな
>
> 強身にて　行き当るをば　下手と知れ  
> 鞠に柳を　上手とぞ云ふ

The mindset for winning by following the enemy. An old poem says:

> When the wind blows,  
> spring passes, following  
> the willow threads  
> as they bend to one side.[^spring-wind]
>
> Know one who meets it with force  
> to be unskilled;  
> in ball-play, the willow  
> is called the skilful one.

## The ultimate principle of iai

> 敵と出合ふ時、討勝と思ふべからず。況や恐るゝ事なし。間に毫末を加へず、電光石火の如く、ちらりと我が心に移る時、無二無三に打込む事、居合の極意也。
>
> 是、柄口六寸の習ひなり。此の間をもって敵の場へ踏み込み打つ也。
>
> 偏に大海を渡るに、陸にて行けば命を失ふ故に、船に乗り行く也。居合柄口六寸の大事、偏に彼の舟に同じ。然れ共、舟にて必ず渡海すと思ふべからず。歌に、
>
> 乗り得ても　心ゆるすな　海士小舟  
> 波間の風の　吹かぬ日ぞなき
>
> 右、波間の風、能々合点しては舟も入らず。歌に、
>
> 有と無しと　堺を渡る　海士小舟  
> 釘も草木も　抜け果てにけり
>
> 此の心にて能々工夫あるべし。口傳。

When encountering an enemy, do not think of striking and winning; still less should you be afraid. Without allowing even a hair’s breadth of interval, when, like lightning and fire struck from flint, **[it] shifts into my mind in a flash**, strike in single-mindedly, without a second or third thought. This is the ultimate principle of iai.[^utsuru]

This is the teaching of 柄口六寸 *tsukaguchi roku-sun*. By means of this **間 *ma***, step into the enemy’s ground and strike.[^ma]

It is just like crossing the great ocean: if one tried to go overland, one would lose one’s life, and therefore one boards a boat. The essential matter of 柄口六寸 in iai is just like that boat. Yet do not suppose that merely because one is aboard a boat one will necessarily cross the sea. A poem says:

> Even once aboard,  
> do not relax your mind  
> in the fisherman’s little boat;  
> there is no day when wind does not blow among the waves.

Yet if, because one fully understands the winds among the waves, one will not even board the boat — a poem says:

> The fisherman’s little boat crosses  
> the boundary between being and non-being;  
> every nail and piece of wood  
> has fallen completely away.[^kusaki]

Investigate this mind thoroughly. Oral transmission.

## Winning without drawing

> 居合抜き覚ると、早く抜かせず。故に詰合の位、能々執行有るべし。只太刀を抜けとばかり思ふべからず。抜かずして勝つの利有り。是、事〔業〕の位に教ゆべし。
>
> 人の我を捕へて抜かせじとする時、必ず抜くまじ。我を捕ゆる人を此方より取り倒すべし。或は先に捕られぬ位有るべし。敵の仕掛により、直ぐに止まり勝つ事もあり、或は其の気を避けて勝つ事も有り。皆、敵の仕掛に寄るなり。工夫有るべし。歌に、
>
> 留ると　思はばそこに　留まれよ  
> 行くと思はば　とくとくと行け
>
> 仇にのみ　人を辛しと　何か思ふ  
> 心よ我を　憂きものと知れ
>
> 右の心にて工夫有るべし。然れ共、剛力、或は色々と理屈を云ふ人あらば、皆我が敵と知れ。然れ共、夫々に心を取られ迷ふ事なかれ。歌に、
>
> 無用なる　手詰の語を　すべからず  
> 無理な人には　勝って利はなし

Once you have learnt to draw in iai, do not be quick to draw. Therefore diligently practise the positions of 詰合 *tsumeai*. Do not think only, “Draw the sword.” There is a principle of **winning without drawing**. This should be taught at the level of the techniques.[^waza]

When someone seizes me and tries to prevent me from drawing, I must not insist upon drawing. Instead, I should take down the one who has seized me. Or there is a position in which I am not seized in the first place. Depending upon the enemy’s attack, there are occasions on which one stops at once and wins, and occasions on which one avoids his *ki* and wins. All depends upon the enemy’s manner of attacking. Investigate this. A poem says:

> If you mean to stop,  
> stop in that very place.  
> If you mean to go,  
> go at once, at once.
>
> Why think only another  
> a cruel adversary?  
> O heart, know that I myself  
> am the troublesome one.

Investigate with this mind. If there are men of brute strength, or men who put forward all manner of arguments, know them all as adversaries. Yet do not let your mind be taken by each of them and fall into confusion. A poem says:

> Do not engage  
> in useless talk of *tezume*;  
> there is no benefit  
> in defeating an unreasonable man.

## 沈成躰 *Chinseitai*

> 敵、太刀打ち難き切って掛るに、沈成躰に勝ち有る事の位にて教へし。工夫有るべし。
>
> 古語に、寸の虫屈むも身を伸びんが為。歌に、
>
> 長からむ　さゝげの花は　短くて  
> 短き栗の　花の長さよ
>
> 右の心にて工夫有るべし。皆、陰合〆、陽に出る位有り。古人も心は〔之〕の内にありとのたまふなり。ものとしたる事に、勝負の位知る事なし。深く工夫有るべし。
>
> 此の沈なる躰心より、よく敵の心見ゆるもの也。
>
> 兵法に曰く、端末未見、人莫能知、と有り。歌に、
>
> 悟り得て　心の花の　開けなば  
> 尋ねん先に　色ぞ染むべき
>
> 霜閉づむ　蓬が下の　きりぎりす  
> 有るかなきかの　聲ぞ聞ゆる

When the enemy cuts in with a blow difficult to meet with the sword, teach the position in which victory lies in **沈成躰 *chinseitai***. Investigate it.[^chinseitai]

An old saying has:

> Even an inchworm bends itself  
> so that it may extend its body.

A poem says:

> The flower of the long cowpea is short,  
> while the flower of the short chestnut is long.

Investigate with this mind. In all things there is a position of gathering and closing yin, then issuing into yang. The ancients too declared that the mind lies within this.[^kore] When a matter has become fixed as a “thing”, one cannot know the position of victory and defeat. Investigate deeply.

From this settled and lowered body-mind, the enemy’s mind becomes clearly visible.

A text on strategy says, “Before the first signs have appeared, no one can know.”[^kanbun] A poem says:

> If, having attained awakening,  
> the flower of the mind should open,  
> before one even seeks it  
> its colour should already appear.
>
> Beneath frost-laden mugwort,  
> the cricket’s voice is heard  
> so faintly one cannot tell  
> whether it is there or not.

## 危道 *Kidō*

> 居合も太刀打も、敵と我と立合ふといなや、危道なくては勝事なし。然れ共、上を打たんとして下を打つ様なる事にてなし。先づ直ぐに行き、間を移し行く危道有り。此の處、詞に出し難し。数月数年、業修錬の功にて合点行くべし。能々日夜修行有るべきなり。
>
> 義経公御歌、
>
> 勢ひ来る　敵にさりなく　出合ふな  
> あひしらひして　潮を抜かせよ

In both iai and sword engagement, the instant the enemy and I face one another, there can be no victory without **危道 *kidō***. Yet this does not mean intending to strike high and then striking low. First go straight ahead; in shifting the interval as you go, there is *kidō*. This point is difficult to put into words. It must be understood through the accomplishment gained in months and years of training in the techniques. Practise it thoroughly, day and night.[^kido]

A poem attributed to Lord Yoshitsune says:

> Do not meet heedlessly  
> the enemy who comes on with force;  
> receive and handle him,  
> and let his tide ebb away.

## Victory before drawing

> 居合と申すは、第一、太刀抜かぬ以前に勝つ事大事也。歌に、
>
> 抜けば切れ　抜かずは切るな　此の刀  
> たゞ切る事に　大事こそあれ
>
> あまたにて　勝れざりしと　聞きしかど  
> 心明剱の　太刀を楽しめ

In iai, the foremost matter is to win before drawing the sword. A poem says:

> If you draw, cut.  
> If you do not draw, do not cut with this sword.  
> It is in cutting itself  
> that the essential matter lies.
>
> Though I have heard that one did not prevail  
> when faced by many,  
> take delight in the sword  
> of **心明剱 *Shinmeiken***, the Sword of the Luminous Mind.[^shinmeiken]

## Drawing while passing

> 行連の太刀抜く事、古人大事に云へり。工夫有るべし。歌に、
>
> 〔夏日向〕　冬日の影と　歩行べし  
> 獨り行くには　障る人なし
>
> 行違ふ　敵の足に　目を付けよ  
> 手はおのづから　留るもの也

The ancients regarded drawing the sword in **行連 *Yukizure*** as important. Investigate it. A poem says:

> Walk as in the summer sunlight  
> and the winter day’s shadow;  
> when one goes alone,  
> there is no one to obstruct the way.[^summer]
>
> Fix your eye upon the feet  
> of the enemy whom you pass;  
> the hands will stop  
> of themselves.

## Drawing when grappled

> 組合の時、太刀抜く様の事。敵組付くと早、我が身に付けて抜く事と知れ。歌に、
>
> 身に付けて　抜く習ひ有る　人はたゞ  
> 組付かぬ間に　切るとこそ聞け
>
> 居合とは　心一つに　定まらず  
> 敵の仕掛を　留る様あり

When grappling, as soon as the enemy closes upon you, know that the way to draw is to draw close against your own body. A poem says:

> Of one who possesses the teaching  
> of drawing close against the body,  
> I hear simply this:  
> cut before he can grapple.
>
> Iai is not fixed  
> in a single state of mind alone;  
> there are ways  
> of stopping the enemy’s attack.[^kokoro-katana]

## When the enemy’s sword is difficult to meet

> 敵、太刀打ち難き我に切って掛るに、早く抜き合せんとすれば、必ず負る事あり。能々工夫有るべし。歌に、
>
> 居合をば　知ったふりして　突かるゝな  
> 抜刀の道を　深く問ふべし
>
> 身の曲尺の　位を深く　習ふべし  
> 留めねど留る　妙ぞ不思議や

When an enemy cuts at me in a manner difficult to meet with the sword, if I try hastily to draw and meet him, there are occasions on which I shall certainly lose. Investigate carefully. A poem says:

> Do not be thrust  
> while pretending that you know iai;  
> inquire deeply  
> into the way of 抜刀 *battō*.[^tsukaruru]
>
> Study deeply  
> the position of the body’s carpenter’s square;  
> though you do not stop it, it stops —  
> a mysterious marvel.[^myo]

## Conclusion of the quoted verses

> 敵色々とありて我を騙すとも、油断する事なかれ。譬へば鞠を蹴るに同じ。我が鞠と人の鞠との色をよく見る事也。
>
> 敵をたゞ　鞠と思ひて　皆人の  
> 詰め開きせば　いかに逃がさん
>
> 本来の　事より出て　事に入り  
> あはれ知らばや　事の深さは
>
> 吹けば鳴る　吹かねば鳴らぬ　笛竹の  
> 聲の良しとは　何を云ふらん
>
> 打解けて　寝るが中なる　心こそ  
> 誠の我を　顕はしにけり
>
> 引寄せて　結べば柴の　庵にて  
> 解れば本の　野原なりけり
>
> 兎に角に　云ふべき様は　なかりけり  
> 九重の塔の　上の足代
>
> 唱ふれば　仏も我も　なかりけり  
> 南無阿弥陀仏の　聲ばかりして
>
> 極楽は　遥かに遠く　聞きしかど  
> 唱へて至る　處なりけり
>
> 居合心持引歌終

The enemy has many colours and may deceive us; never be careless. It is like kicking a ball: distinguish carefully the colour of your own ball from that of the other person.

> Think of the enemy simply as a ball;  
> if you close and open the interval against every man,  
> how could any escape?
>
> Coming forth from the original matter  
> and entering into particular matters —  
> ah, if only one might know  
> the depth of the matter.
>
> Blow it and the bamboo flute sounds;  
> do not blow it and it does not.  
> What, then, can one mean  
> by calling its voice good?
>
> The mind present  
> while one sleeps completely at ease —  
> that has revealed  
> the true self.
>
> Draw the brushwood together and bind it,  
> and it becomes a hut;  
> untie it,  
> and it is the original field once more.
>
> In the end there is nothing whatever  
> that can be put into words:  
> the scaffolding  
> atop a nine-storey pagoda.
>
> When one chants,  
> neither Buddha nor self remains;  
> there is only the voice  
> saying *Namu Amida Butsu*.[^tonaureba]
>
> Though I had heard  
> that the Pure Land was far away,  
> it is a place one reaches  
> through chanting.[^kikishi]

**End of the quoted verses on the mindset of iai.**

## Textual notes

[^kake]: K reads 敵の**掛**を知らする. S reads 敵の**勝**を知らする, “makes the enemy’s victory known”. 掛 is retained because it is clear in K. The exact martial sense remains open: the enemy’s coming-on, engagement, attack or initiative are all possible.

[^spring-wind]: S introduces the passage as 春風を切ると云ふ, “called cutting the spring wind”, and reads 廻る春かな where K appears to have 過ぐる春かな.

[^utsuru]: K reads 我が心に**移る**時, whereas S reads 我が心に**勝つ**時. The parallel 居合心立合之大事 has 敵打出す所にてちらりと気移りして勝事なり, which supports an interpretation involving the transfer or apprehension of the enemy’s *ki* or decisive cue. It does not, however, supply the omitted grammatical subject with certainty.

[^ma]: K reads 此の**間**をもって. S has 此の**習**を以て, “by means of this teaching”. S also includes an additional sentence — 然れ共只切込ていなし是に柄口六寸の習なり — which is absent from K.

[^kusaki]: K clearly prints 釘も**草木**も, literally “nails and grasses/trees”; “wood” is a cautious contextual rendering. S reads 釘も**楔**も, “nails and wedges”, which is semantically more natural. The inherited reading cannot yet be decided.

[^waza]: K is unclear at 是事さの位. S gives 是事（業）の位. The translation follows 業 *waza* provisionally.

[^chinseitai]: S includes an explanatory annotation absent from K: 沈成躰 means lowering one’s body against the enemy and settling one’s *ki*.

[^kore]: The relevant character in K is difficult to read. S has 心は**之**内に有り, “the mind lies within this”.

[^kanbun]: K and S preserve a damaged or compressed kanbun phrase. The translation gives the probable sense rather than a secure grammatical reconstruction.

[^kido]: K reads 危道, 業 and 義経公. S has 是道, 事 and 義公. Mitsuhira independently identifies the former three as readings in the Kimura Eijū witness. 危道 is therefore retained as *kidō*. The following sentence excludes a simple high-low feint, but does not justify silently changing the graph to 詭道, “the deceptive way”.

[^shinmeiken]: The exact construction of the preceding line is uncertain. 心明剱 is retained as the named principle *Shinmeiken* and glossed literally.

[^summer]: K clearly prints 夏日なく, which appears corrupt in context. S reads 夏日向, followed by 冬日の影. The normalised Japanese and translation provisionally follow S; the brackets mark the emendation.

[^kokoro-katana]: K reads 心一つ, “one mind” or “a single state of mind”. S and parallel song traditions read 刀一つ, “the sword alone”. The readings yield materially different teachings, so neither has been silently substituted for the other.

[^tsukaruru]: K has 突かるゝな, “do not be thrust”, and 抜刀の道. S has つかるゝな and 居合の道. K is followed here.

[^myo]: K reads 妙ぞ不思議や, “a mysterious marvel”. S reads 事ぞふしぎや, “what a mysterious thing”. K is followed here.

[^tonaureba]: K prints とのおれば, which is corrupt in context. S and the following 南無阿弥陀仏 establish 唱ふれば with high confidence.

[^kikishi]: K reads 聞きしかど, “though I had heard”. S reads ゆきしかど, “though [I] went”, which is less coherent in the poem.
